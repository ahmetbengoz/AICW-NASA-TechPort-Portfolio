# Version 3 scientific scope and validation audit

The current contribution is a retrospective empirical audit of the
importance-to-ranking conversion. Normalising importance is not claimed as a
new AI method or a preference estimator. No acceptance probability or simulated
editorial endorsement is offered as scientific evidence.

New results are generated by `scripts/reproduce_all.py`: training-only nested
weight learning, structural prediction ablations, conventional coefficient
comparators, direct-prediction ranking, 10/20/30% cohort shortlists, fractional
ties, programme/start-date transport and label randomisation. Table summaries
are in `outputs/validation_extension/`.

Key findings are a strong gain-only structural baseline and no consistent
shortlist advantage from converted importance. Under attainment, direct
prediction and converted scores can have opposite lift on the same random
outer cohorts. Some chronological results favour converted weights; these
counter-results remain visible. No universal preferred policy follows.

Remaining limitations are the nonrandom 201-project completed-case batch,
absence of independent portfolios and historical field versions, coarse TRL
proxies, unsigned illustrative value directions and no causal decision utility.

Current literature already contains ML–MCDA weighting integrations, local
perturbation analyses and general feature-importance auditing. The article
therefore cites these foundations and evaluates a narrower engineering
application, without claiming the first general audit protocol. Bibliographic
metadata are checked in `reference_audit.json`.
